# Executive Decision Brief: Durable Work

## The decision in plain language

A workflow is a promise that remains open after the first request ends. The
investment question is whether the organization can preserve ownership,
progress, deadlines, recovery, and proof until that promise reaches an
acceptable ending.

## What this can unlock

- automation of work that spans hours, weeks, systems, or human judgment;
- fewer obligations lost between teams or hidden inside retries;
- safer use of AI for consequential, long-running work;
- operational evidence about delay, residual exposure, and completion.

## What it protects

It limits the chance that a technically successful step is mistaken for a
fulfilled customer or regulatory obligation. It does not eliminate failure or
guarantee a business result.

## Questions before approval

1. What promise remains open, and to whom?
2. Who owns it after the initiating system, team, or model session ends?
3. Which effects cannot simply be rolled back?
4. What deadlines force escalation or a different ending?
5. What evidence proves closure rather than activity?
6. What exposure remains, and who accepted it?

## Evidence packet

Require a responsibility-and-progress brief, one exercised failure path,
closure and reconciliation evidence, an operating owner, and a named trigger
for reconsideration.

## Decision options

Approve a bounded pilot, approve with conditions, redesign the obligation or
boundary, keep human ownership, or stop. “The workflow ran” is not enough to
justify expansion.
