# Team Workshop: Keep One Promise Across Time and Failure

Use this 75-minute session before choosing or expanding a workflow engine.

## Outcome

The team leaves with one reviewable long-running promise, a first failure map,
an accountable owner, and one evidence-producing experiment.

## Participants

Bring the service or product owner, a developer, operations, and one person who
owns a human decision, downstream effect, or customer consequence.

## Agenda

### 0–10 minutes — State the promise

Complete the six-line first pass in the
[Responsibility-and-Progress Brief](workflow-responsibility-and-progress-brief.md).
Do not begin with workflow steps.

### 10–25 minutes — Draw progress and ownership

For each state, name who can advance it, which clock matters, and which state
store remains authoritative after a process or model session ends.

### 25–40 minutes — Disturb the happy path

Choose a timeout, duplicated request, partial effect, expired approval, or
release during open work. Complete the matching row in the
[Compensation Matrix](compensation-and-failure-matrix.md).

### 40–55 minutes — Define proof

Separate attempt evidence from business-outcome evidence. State what proves the
beneficiary received an acceptable ending and how reconciliation finds work
that is neither advancing nor closed.

### 55–65 minutes — Translate value

Complete one row of the [Value and Evidence Ledger](VALUE-AND-EVIDENCE-LEDGER.md).
Name delay, rework, exposure, or customer harm without inventing a financial
result.

### 65–75 minutes — Assign the next test

Name the owner, test date, observed evidence, and rule that will change the
design. Use the [Pilot and Usability Route](PILOT-AND-USABILITY.md) if testing
the assets with practitioners.

## Read-back test

Ask a participant outside engineering to explain the open promise, the owner
after failure, and the evidence of closure. Record the words that caused
confusion; do not translate for the template.
